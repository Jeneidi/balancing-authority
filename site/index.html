The baseline is the part that makes the result credible.

Utilities live by day-ahead load forecasts. This project does not compare a model with a strawman. It compares one LightGBM model with the forecast the balancing authority actually published.

What it proves

Every prediction uses calendar features, demand from at least a day earlier, same-hour-last-week demand, and the temperature forecast issued the day before. The leakage guard is executable, not a promise.

The same discipline transfers to LLM agents, RAG, evals and MCP tooling: log the source, freeze the evaluation window, and show where the system loses. For grid work, the next questions are anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, smart meters and NRC licensing traceability.

D2 LEAKAGE-SAFE FORECASTING

How it works

One model, two honest baselines, one walk-forward evaluation.

  1. Get public inputs

    Read EIA-930 demand and operator forecasts for 2024 through 2026 H1. Fetch archived day-ahead temperature from Open-Meteo.

  2. Build the next-day row

    Use calendar features, demand lags and the previous-day weather forecast. No actual weather is allowed into the feature row.

  3. Train without shuffling

    Train on 2024 and 2025 H1, validate on 2025 H2, then score the unseen 2026 H1 test window.

  4. Show the miss

    Report MAPE, bootstrap confidence intervals, win rate, worst days and the share of reality inside the 90% band.
